GROG

Grog is a ceramic display typeface whose letterforms are shaped by the kiln. Each character carries the weight of unfired clay — rough at the edges, geometric at the core, structured like a cross-section of stacked kiln shelves. The forms are drawn from the physical logic of ceramics: thick walls, compressed joints, material memory. Grog does not perform elegance. It performs permanence.

Two versions exist. Regular renders the typeface in its raw state — clay-toned, heavy, unglazed. Color applies post-firing glaze across the letterforms, a warm spectrum running from iron red through ash to celadon. The same letter, two temperatures, two results. Neither is more correct than the other.

Grog is designed in the spirit of quiet studio craft. No ornament. No flourish. Only the mark left by material and heat. The typeface is best used large, unhurried, and with space to breathe — the way a finished piece sits on a shelf before anyone decides what it means.
